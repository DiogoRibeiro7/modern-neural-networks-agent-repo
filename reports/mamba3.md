## 1. Claim level

**`educational implementation`** — see [`docs/claim_policy.md`](../docs/claim_policy.md).

This is the first track whose primary source was retrieved and read inside this
environment. The recurrence, the discretization coefficients, and both rotation
formulations are transcribed from the paper's Propositions 1-4 and equations (1)-(14), and
the implementation is checked against them in the tests. What is *not* claimed: any
reproduction of the paper's results, any evaluation of its systems claims, and any
language-modelling evidence. There is no fused kernel and no parallel scan.

See [`docs/source_registry.md`](../docs/source_registry.md#track-03--mamba-3) for the full

## 3. Method

Full formulation, shape table, equation-to-code mapping, complexity, and the six recorded
deviations are in
[`src/modern_nn_lab/tracks/mamba3/README.md`](../src/modern_nn_lab/tracks/mamba3/README.md).

**Fairness controls.**

1. One shared training loop for every architecture.
2. Baseline widths are searched to match the SSM's parameter count.
3. Each architecture selects its learning rate from the same grid on validation. The test
   split is scored once.
4. Each ablation changes exactly one flag, and the tests assert that switching it off
   recovers the prior method *exactly* rather than approximately.
5. The tasks are generated with the same parameters and the same seed as Track 02, so the
   xLSTM records are directly comparable without rerunning them.
6. Every seed is recorded; none is dropped.

## 8. Failures and instabilities

**No run diverged.** All 105 records have `status="success"`.

**No implementation bug survived to the experiments.** Unlike Track 02, the invariant
tests here passed on the first complete run. That is attributable to the test design
rather than to luck: because each of the three mechanisms has a flag that must recover a
*previously published* method exactly, three of the tests are equality assertions against
formulas transcribed from the paper (Table 1's exponential-Euler row, Proposition 1's
coefficients, equations 12-14's rank decomposition), and one asserts the paper's own
Proposition 2 ⟺ Proposition 3 identity. Those are much stronger constraints than "the
loss goes down".

**Where the mechanism fails.**

- The full model does not solve parity reliably: 0.847 ± 0.17, against an LSTM's
  1.000 ± 0.000 with **fewer** parameters (3952 vs 3482). The seed spread is large, so
  some seeds find the rotational solution and others do not.
- MIMO is a net loss here. The rank-1 ablation matches or beats the rank-2 default on all
  three tasks while carrying ~1000 fewer parameters. This is not a contradiction of the
  source, which motivates MIMO by decode-time arithmetic intensity on an accelerator — a
  quantity this setup cannot measure at all.
- The Transformer again wins the copy task outright (0.898 vs the best SSM's 0.445).

**Ablations are not parameter-matched to the full model**, because each one deletes the
parameters belonging to the mechanism it removes (the λ projection, the θ projection, the
rank-2 `B`/`C` columns). The gaps are 5-25 %. Where this matters — the parity result — the
confound is resolved in the same way as in Track 02: the LSTM solves the task perfectly
with fewer parameters than either variant, so capacity cannot be the explanation.


## 9. Interpretation

**1. The source's central theoretical claim about rotation is confirmed, on a
pre-registered test.** The paper argues that a real non-negative transition cannot
represent parity, and that a rotational one can. State tracking *is* parity, and the
ablation was declared in the suite docstring before the run:

| state tracking | accuracy | 95 % CI | params |
|---|---|---|---|
| mamba3 (rotary) | **0.847 ± 0.17** | [0.716, 0.979] | 3952 |
| mamba3 `no-rotation` | **0.578 ± 0.023** | [0.560, 0.597] | 3688 |
| Transformer | 0.563 ± 0.019 | [0.550, 0.580] | 4746 |
| LSTM | 1.000 ± 0.000 | [1, 1] | 3482 |

The intervals for the two SSM variants are disjoint. Removing rotation collapses the model
to 0.578 — statistically indistinguishable from the Transformer, and barely above the 0.5
chance level. Note also the *tightness* of the ablated interval (±0.023): it is not
failing erratically, it is reliably unable to do the task. That is the signature of a
representational limit rather than an optimization one.

**2. This resolves the failure Track 02 left open, on identical data.** The xLSTM track
found that its mLSTM configuration could not solve parity (0.589 ± 0.011), and attributed
it to the state update being affine in the state. Track 03 runs the *same task, same
seed, same protocol* and shows that adding rotation to an otherwise comparable linear
recurrence lifts it to 0.847. Two independent linear-recurrence architectures, one
mechanism difference, the predicted direction:

| same task, same data | parity accuracy |
|---|---|
| xLSTM mLSTM (affine state update) | 0.589 ± 0.011 |
| Mamba-3 without rotation | 0.578 ± 0.023 |
| Mamba-3 with rotation | 0.847 ± 0.17 |

The two non-rotational linear recurrences land in the same place despite coming from
different papers, which is what a shared representational ceiling looks like.

**3. Exponential-trapezoidal discretization shows a positive but weak effect.** Against
the Euler ablation: 0.847 vs 0.641 on parity and 0.363 vs 0.275 on selective recall, but
the intervals overlap in both cases, and copy shows nothing (0.407 vs 0.413). The honest
verdict is *suggestive, not established at this scale*. This is the expected outcome: the
trapezoidal rule improves the truncation error of the state-input integral from `O(Δ³)` to
`O(Δ⁴)`, and with `Δ` initialized in `[10⁻³, 10⁻¹]` and sequences of 10-17 steps, there is
little error for a better integrator to recover. Testing it where `Δ` is large is listed
as the next experiment.

**4. MIMO buys nothing measurable and costs parameters.** Rank 1 versus rank 2: 0.445 vs
0.407 on copy, 0.363 vs 0.363 on recall, 0.686 vs 0.847 on parity — no consistent
direction, and rank 1 uses ~20 % fewer parameters. This is not evidence against the
source. Its Table 2 motivates MIMO by raising decode arithmetic intensity from `Θ(1)` to
`Θ(R)` on hardware whose decode is memory-bound; on a CPU running a Python scan there is
no memory-bound decode to improve. **The correct conclusion is that this track cannot
evaluate contribution 3, not that contribution 3 does not work.**

**5. The linear-time claim is visible in wall clock — for the SSM only.** Latency grows
7.9× as sequence length grows 8× (13.4 ms → 105.6 ms), which is linear scaling to within
measurement error, exactly as an SSM's `O(T·N·P·R)` predicts. The baselines' rows are
uninformative: at this width their fixed per-call overhead swamps their per-token work, so
their timings wander non-monotonically. The one thing this table does *not* show is
attention's quadratic cost — `T = 128` is far too short for that, and saying otherwise
would be exactly the kind of throughput claim the benchmark rules forbid.

**6. On absolute quality the SSM is not the best model on any of the three tasks.**
Transformer wins copy, LSTM wins parity, and selective recall does not separate anything
(every architecture sits between 0.21 and 0.36 with overlapping intervals, as in Track 02).
The value of this track is not a leaderboard position; it is that a specific theoretical
claim about representational capacity was stated by the source, encoded as an ablation,
and measured.


## 10. Limitations

1. **No systems evidence at all.** The source's third contribution is motivated entirely
   by arithmetic intensity during memory-bound decode on an H100. This implementation
   steps the recurrence in Python on a CPU, so that claim is not merely unverified here —
   it is unverifiable here.
2. **No language modelling**, so nothing bears on the paper's reported 1.5B-scale results.
3. **Short sequences** (T = 10 to 17). The trapezoidal rule reduces truncation error in
   `Δ`, and a second-order gain is easiest to see when `Δ` is not already small.
4. **A stripped-down block** (deviation 2): no gated MLP, no short causal convolution, no
   in-block normalization. The measured numbers are for the recurrence, not for a
   Mamba-3 layer as the paper defines it.
5. **Small state.** `state_size = 8` per head with `d_model = 32` is far below any regime
   where MIMO rank or state capacity would plausibly matter.

## 11. Next experiments

1. Implement the chunked/SSD parallel form and assert it equals the sequential scan to
   numerical tolerance, then report throughput honestly.
2. Sweep `Δ` magnitude to find the regime where second-order discretization actually pays,
   rather than testing it only where `Δ` is small.
3. Extend the parity task to longer sequences and to modulus > 2, where the rotation angle
   must be `2π/k` rather than `π`.
4. Sweep MIMO rank against state size on a task with enough capacity pressure for either
   to matter.
5. Add the block components from deviation 2 one at a time, to separate the recurrence's
   contribution from the surrounding architecture's.
