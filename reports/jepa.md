## 3. What exactly is predicted

Full detail is in
[`src/modern_nn_lab/tracks/jepa/README.md`](../src/modern_nn_lab/tracks/jepa/README.md).

Patches are split into a visible **context** set and a hidden **target** set. The context
encoder embeds the visible patches and pools them; the predictor produces each hidden
patch's **representation**, never its observed values:

```
ẑ_p = g_φ( mean of f_θ(context patches) )
z_p = stopgrad( f_ξ(x_p) )                    ξ = EMA of θ
L   = mean over hidden p of ‖ẑ_p − z_p‖²
```

Nothing in the loss mentions the observations. The model is therefore free to discard
anything about a patch that another patch could not have predicted — which, by construction
of the dataset, is exactly the nuisance.

## 4. Why trivial collapse is or is not prevented

**The loss alone does not prevent it.** If `f(x) = k` for every input, the predictor outputs
`k` and the loss is exactly zero. This is asserted, not assumed:
`test_a_constant_encoder_achieves_zero_loss`. A low training loss is not evidence of a good
representation here, and can be evidence of the opposite.

**What prevents it** is a stop-gradient paired with a target encoder whose weights are an
exponential moving average of the online encoder's. No gradient reaches the target, so the
loss cannot reduce itself by moving the target; the online encoder chases a lagged copy of
itself. The tests assert the structural part — the EMA target's parameters carry
`requires_grad=False` and its outputs do not require grad, while the ablation's targets do.

**This is an empirical stabilizer, not a proof.** Collapse remains reachable in principle,
so "collapse did not occur" is reported below as a measurement rather than a guarantee. The
`jepa-none` row exists precisely so the failure is shown rather than described.

## 5. Reading the collapse metrics

**Effective rank does not detect total collapse**, and the reason is easy to miss. When an
encoder becomes constant, what remains is floating-point noise — which is *isotropic*, so
the covariance has roughly equal eigenvalues and the effective rank comes out **high**. In
the measurements below the collapsed model reports a healthy-looking normalized effective
rank while its standard deviation is near zero. Reading the rank alone would have called it
healthy.

Variance has the opposite blind spot: it misses *dimensional* collapse, where every sample
lies on one line with healthy per-dimension spread.

So both are reported, always, standard deviation first — and the `collapsed` column is
derived from the standard deviation, not the rank.
`test_effective_rank_does_not_detect_total_collapse` pins the trap.

## 10. Limitations

- **The dataset is synthetic and the factor structure is the one this repository chose.**
  Content shared across patches and nuisance drawn per patch is exactly the structure a
  predictive objective is suited to. That makes the task a clean test of the mechanism and a
  poor guide to how it behaves where the split is not so tidy.
- **MLP encoders on unordered patches**, not a vision transformer on images. No positional
  structure, no multi-block masking, no rollouts.
- **Seed variance covers training only.** One dataset is generated from a fixed seed and
  shared by every model, so the intervals describe initialization and optimization noise.
- **`raw-features` is a strong floor here** and the learned models are not asked to beat it
  on an external task — there is no downstream task in this track beyond the probes.
- **The masking and predictor sweeps are single-seed**, so they show direction rather than a
  confidence interval.
- **The variance-hinge and EMA variants are not tuned against each other.** Each uses one
  setting of its own hyperparameter, so the comparison is between two configurations, not
  between two mechanisms at their best.

## 11. Next experiments

1. Re-run the sweeps across seeds, so §7 and §8 carry intervals rather than points.
2. Sweep the EMA decay and the variance floor jointly. The current comparison fixes both,
   and either mechanism could look better or worse at a different setting.
3. Add a factor structure where content and nuisance are *correlated*, which is the case the
   clean split here deliberately avoids and the one real data always presents.
4. Add a downstream task distinct from the probes — the probes recover the generative
   factors, which is the friendliest possible evaluation of a representation.
